**らしい** has two different uses:

1. **Inference from hearsay**: "apparently", "I hear", "it seems". The speaker passes on information from an indirect source, without taking full responsibility for it.
2. **Typical quality** (noun + らしい): "typical of", "befitting", "-like". 男らしい = manly; 春らしい = spring-like.

## Formation

| Before | Example |
| ------ | ------- |
| Verb | 来るらしい, 来たらしい |
| I-adjective | 高いらしい |
| Na-adjective | 静からしい (no だ) |
| Noun | 学生らしい (no だ) |

らしい conjugates like an **i-adjective**: らしくない, らしかった, らしく.

## "Apparently" examples

- 明日は雪が降るらしい。 — Apparently it's going to snow tomorrow.
- 田中さんは会社を辞めたらしいよ。 — I hear Mr. Tanaka quit his job.
- あの店は今月で閉店するらしいです。 — It seems that shop is closing at the end of the month.
- 隣の家に誰か引っ越してきたらしい。 — It seems someone has moved in next door.

## "Typical of" examples

- 今日は春らしい暖かい日ですね。 — It's a warm, spring-like day today.
- 文句を言うなんて、君らしくないね。 — Complaining like that isn't like you.
- もっと学生らしい服を着なさい。 — Wear clothes more befitting a student.

## The two meanings with nouns

With nouns, the two uses can look identical:

| Sentence | Meaning |
| -------- | ------- |
| あの人は学生らしい。 | Apparently that person is a student. (hearsay) |
| あの人はとても学生らしい。 | That person is very student-like. (typical) |

Words like とても or the context decide. Typical-quality らしい is often a fixed word: 男らしい, 女らしい, 子供らしい, 自分らしい (true to oneself).

## らしい vs そうだ vs ようだ

- **そうだ (hearsay):** directly reporting what you heard, fairly confident about the source.
- **らしい:** based on rumours or indirect information; the speaker keeps some distance and isn't sure.
- **ようだ / みたい:** the speaker's own judgement from what they observe.

In practice らしい often sounds like "word is...", "apparently...", while ようだ sounds like "it looks to me as if...".

## Pitfalls

- No だ before らしい: 学生らしい, not 学生だらしい; 静からしい, not 静かだらしい.
- Typical-quality らしい only attaches to nouns, not to verbs.
- Don't use らしい about your own feelings or actions; you know them directly.
- Because らしい passes on unconfirmed information, it can sound like gossip when used about people.
