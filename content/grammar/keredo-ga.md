**けれど** (and its variants けど, けれども) and **が** join two clauses with "but" or "although". They attach to the end of the first clause.

## Formation

| Form | Register | Example |
| ---- | -------- | ------- |
| けど | casual speech | 高いけど、買う |
| けれど | neutral | 高いけれど、買う |
| けれども | more formal | 高いですけれども、買います |
| が | neutral to formal, common in writing | 高いですが、買います |

They follow the plain or polite form: 行くけど, 行きますけど, 学生だけど, 学生ですが. Unlike ので, だ stays: 静かだが, 学生だけど.

## Examples

- このかばんは高いけど、とても丈夫です。 — This bag is expensive, but it's very sturdy.
- 日本語は難しいですが、面白いです。 — Japanese is difficult, but it's interesting.
- 行きたいけど、時間がない。 — I want to go, but I don't have time.
- 彼は若いが、経験が豊富だ。 — He is young, but very experienced.
- すみません、ちょっと聞きたいことがあるんですけど…。 — Excuse me, there's something I'd like to ask...
- もしもし、田中ですが、山田さんはいらっしゃいますか。 — Hello, this is Tanaka. Is Ms. Yamada there?
- 昨日の映画、見たけど、あまり面白くなかった。 — I saw that film yesterday, but it wasn't very good.

## Softening: the "trailing" けど

A very common use is not contrast at all. けど / が at the end of a sentence, or before the real point, softens a statement or request and leaves room for the listener. 明日はちょっと用事があるんですけど… (I have something on tomorrow, so...) is a polite refusal; 田中ですが… on the phone just means "It's Tanaka (calling)...".

Here けど and が work like a pause: "I'm telling you this as background". Leaving a sentence unfinished with けど is polite, not rude.

## が vs けど

The meanings are the same. が sounds more formal and written; けど is everyday speech. In polite speech, ですが and ですけど are both common, with ですが slightly more formal.

## Sentence-initial forms

To start a new sentence with "but", use でも (casual or neutral), しかし (formal, written), or だけど (casual): 美味しかった。でも、高かった.

## Pitfalls

- Do not confuse conjunction が (after a clause) with the subject particle が (after a noun). 雨が降っているが、出かける has both.
- けど is not used to link two nouns. "Cats but not dogs" is 猫は好きだけど、犬は好きじゃない.
- For "even though" with surprise or frustration, use [noni](#/grammar/noni) instead.
