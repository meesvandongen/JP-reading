**か** is the question particle. Put it at the end of a polite sentence and it becomes a question; no change in word order is needed. It also means "or" between nouns, and turns question words into "some-" words.

## Uses

| Use | Example | Meaning |
| --- | ------- | ------- |
| Yes/no question | 学生ですか。 | Are you a student? |
| Question-word question | 何を食べますか。 | What will you eat? |
| "Or" between nouns | 電車かバス | train or bus |
| Embedded question | 何時に来るか分からない | I don't know what time he'll come |
| "Whether or not" | 行くかどうか | whether (I'll) go or not |
| Question word + か | 何か / 誰か / どこか | something / someone / somewhere |
| Invitation | 行きませんか。 | Won't you come? |

## Examples

- すみません、トイレはどこですか。 — Excuse me, where is the toilet?
- 一緒に映画を見に行きませんか。 — Would you like to go and see a film together?
- お茶かコーヒーを飲みますか。 — Would you like tea or coffee?
- 何か飲みたいです。 — I want to drink something.
- 誰か来ましたよ。 — Someone has come.
- 明日の会議が何時に始まるか知っていますか。 — Do you know what time tomorrow's meeting starts?
- パーティーに行くかどうか、まだ決めていません。 — I haven't decided yet whether I'll go to the party.

## Questions in casual speech

In plain (casual) speech, か is usually **dropped**; a rising tone is enough: 行く？ (Are you going?), 食べた？ (Did you eat?). Casual questions also often end in の: どこ行くの？ Plain-form + か can sound rough or masculine (行くか？), and after a noun you cannot say だか; just say 学生？

## Embedded questions

A question inside a bigger sentence takes か after the plain form: どこに住んでいるか教えてください (Please tell me where you live). For yes/no questions, use かどうか ("whether or not"). After nouns and na-adjectives, だ is dropped before か: 本当か分からない (I don't know if it's true).

## Question word + か vs + も

| | + か (some-) | + も + negative (no-) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 何 | 何か (something) | 何も (nothing) |
| 誰 | 誰か (someone) | 誰も (nobody) |
| どこ | どこか (somewhere) | どこにも (nowhere) |
| いつ | いつか (someday) | (いつも = always) |

## Pitfalls

- Japanese uses 。 at the end of polite か questions in formal writing, though ？ is common in casual text.
- か at the end with falling intonation can mean realization: そうですか (Oh, I see).
