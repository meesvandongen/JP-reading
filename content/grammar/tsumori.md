**つもり** is a noun meaning "intention". After the dictionary form, it means "intend to", "plan to": 行くつもりです (I intend to go). It expresses a fairly firm, already decided plan.

## Formation

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Dictionary form + つもりだ | intend to | 行くつもりだ |
| Nai-form + つもりだ | intend not to | 行かないつもりだ |
| Dictionary form + つもりはない | have no intention of | 行くつもりはない |
| Ta-form + つもり | believe one did | 言ったつもり |
| Noun + の + つもり | as if it were, thinking of it as | 冗談のつもり |

Polite: つもりです. Past: つもりだった (I had intended to).

## Examples

- 来年、日本へ留学するつもりです。 — I plan to study abroad in Japan next year.
- 今日はお酒を飲まないつもりだ。 — I don't intend to drink today.
- 仕事を辞めるつもりはありません。 — I have no intention of quitting my job.
- 夏休みに何をするつもりですか。 — What are you planning to do in the summer holidays?
- 早く帰るつもりだったが、残業になってしまった。 — I had meant to go home early, but I ended up doing overtime.
- ちゃんと説明したつもりですが、分かりにくかったですか。 — I thought I'd explained it properly; was it hard to follow?
- 冗談のつもりで言ったんだ。 — I meant it as a joke.

## つもり vs volitional + と思う

| つもり | ようと思う |
| ------ | ---------- |
| firm, decided plan | idea being considered, or a decision just made |
| 結婚するつもりだ | 結婚しようと思う |

## ないつもり vs つもりはない

| Phrase | Meaning |
| ------ | ------- |
| 行かないつもりだ | I plan not to go (a plan). |
| 行くつもりはない | I have no intention of going (a stronger, sometimes defiant denial). |

## "I thought I did": ta-form + つもり

With the past tense, つもり describes what the speaker **believed** was the case, often when reality turned out different: 鍵をかけたつもりだった (I thought I'd locked it). Similarly: 若いつもりでいる (thinks of himself as young).

## Pitfalls

- Asking a superior about their plans with つもり (先生はどうするつもりですか) can sound confrontational, like "What do you think you're doing?". Use ご予定は？ instead.
- For fixed schedules (timetables, official plans), use 予定 rather than つもり: 会議は三時に始まる予定です.
- つもり is a noun, so it can take particles: つもりで, つもりは, つもりもない.
