**は** marks the **topic**: what the sentence is about. Think of it as "as for X, ...". Although it is written with the hiragana は (ha), as a particle it is always pronounced **wa**.

The topic is often, but not always, the grammatical subject. Once a topic is set, Japanese speakers usually stop repeating it, so は can carry over several sentences.

## Formation

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Noun + は | 私は | as for me |
| Noun + particle + は | 東京には | as for in Tokyo |
| Time + は | 今日は | as for today |

は replaces が and を, but it stacks after other particles: には, では, とは, からは.

## Examples

- 私は田中です。 — I'm Tanaka.
- 今日は暑いですね。 — It's hot today, isn't it?
- 象は鼻が長い。 — Elephants have long trunks. (As for elephants, the trunk is long.)
- この本はもう読みました。 — I've already read this book.
- 肉は食べますが、魚は食べません。 — I eat meat, but I don't eat fish.
- 日本には山が多い。 — There are many mountains in Japan.
- 「お名前は？」「山本です。」 — "Your name?" "It's Yamamoto."

## Contrast

A second important job of は is **contrast**. When two things are compared, both get は: 肉は食べますが、魚は食べません. Even a single は can hint at an unspoken contrast: 日本語は話せます ("Japanese, I can speak", implying maybe not other languages).

This is also why は appears so often in negative sentences: ビールは飲みません suggests "beer, I don't drink (other things maybe)".

## は vs が

This is the classic beginner question. A rough guide:

- は presents **known** information as the topic, and the focus is on what comes **after** it.
- が marks the subject when the focus is on the subject **itself**, often new information.

Compare 田中さんは来ました (Tanaka came, talking about Tanaka) with 田中さんが来ました (It was Tanaka who came / Tanaka has arrived). Question words like 誰 and 何 can never take は; you must say 誰が来ましたか. See [Particle が](#/grammar/particle-ga) for more.

## Pitfalls

- In subordinate clauses, the subject takes が, not は: 私が作ったケーキ (the cake I made), not 私は作ったケーキ.
- Do not overuse 私は. Once it is clear you are talking about yourself, drop it.
- Writing わ instead of は is a spelling mistake; わ is a different, sentence-final particle.
