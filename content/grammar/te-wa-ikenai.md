**てはいけない** means "must not" or "may not". Literally it is "if you do it, it won't go (well)". It states a rule or strong prohibition. It is the negative counterpart of [てもいい](#/grammar/te-mo-ii).

## Formation

Te-form + は + いけない. In speech, ては contracts to **ちゃ** and では to **じゃ**.

| | Formal | Casual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 食べる | 食べてはいけない | 食べちゃいけない / 食べちゃだめ |
| 飲む | 飲んではいけない | 飲んじゃいけない / 飲んじゃだめ |
| 行く | 行ってはいけない | 行っちゃだめ |
| Polite | 食べてはいけません | |

You can also use **だめ** (no good) or the formal **なりません** in place of いけない: 入ってはだめ, 入ってはなりません.

## Examples

- ここでたばこを吸ってはいけません。 — You must not smoke here.
- 美術館の中で写真を撮ってはいけない。 — You may not take photos inside the museum.
- 試験中に話してはいけません。 — You must not talk during the exam.
- この川で泳いではいけないよ。 — You mustn't swim in this river, you know.
- 「これ、食べてもいい？」「だめ！食べちゃだめ！」 — "Can I eat this?" "No! Don't eat it!"
- 人の悪口を言ってはいけないと母によく言われた。 — My mother often told me I shouldn't speak ill of others.
- 夜遅くまでゲームしちゃだめだよ。 — Don't play games until late at night.

## Who uses it?

てはいけない is direct. It is used for:

- Rules and laws: signs, school rules, instructions.
- Parents and teachers speaking to children.
- General moral statements.

Using it to a superior or customer sounds like you are scolding them. In polite situations, use ないでください or a softer phrase: こちらではご遠慮ください (Please refrain here).

## Saying "may I?" and getting a "no"

Typical exchange:

「ここに座ってもいいですか。」「すみません、そこはちょっと…。」

Answering with 座ってはいけません is correct but stiff and strong.

## Related: なければならない

Adding ない gives a double negative meaning "must": 行かなくてはいけない (if I don't go, it won't do = I must go). See [Nakereba naranai](#/grammar/nakereba-naranai).

## Pitfalls

- The は in ては is the particle, pronounced **wa**: tabete wa ikenai.
- With voiced te-forms (で), you get では → じゃ: 飲んじゃだめ, not 飲んちゃだめ.
- ないでください (please don't) is a request, while てはいけない states a rule.
