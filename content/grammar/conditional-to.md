The conditional **と** links a condition to a result that follows **automatically** or **inevitably**: "whenever X happens, Y happens", "if you do X, Y (always) results". It is used for natural laws, habits, machines, directions and discoveries.

## Formation

Plain non-past form + と.

| Before | Example |
| ------ | ------- |
| Verb | 押すと, 押さないと |
| I-adjective | 寒いと |
| Na-adjective | 静かだと |
| Noun | 雨だと |

The clause before と is almost always **non-past**, even when the whole sentence is in the past.

## Examples

- 春になると、桜が咲きます。 — When spring comes, the cherry blossoms bloom.
- このボタンを押すと、お湯が出ます。 — If you press this button, hot water comes out.
- 二に三を足すと、五になる。 — Two plus three makes five.
- まっすぐ行くと、右に郵便局があります。 — Go straight and you'll see the post office on the right.
- 私はコーヒーを飲むと、眠れなくなる。 — Whenever I drink coffee, I can't sleep.
- 早く行かないと、遅れるよ。 — If you don't go soon, you'll be late.
- 窓を開けると、雪が降っていた。 — When I opened the window, (I found) it was snowing.

## Discovery in the past

With a past-tense main clause, と can describe something you **discovered** or something that happened right after: 家に帰ると、母が料理をしていた (When I got home, my mother was cooking). This storytelling use is common in novels.

## The big restriction

Because と expresses automatic results, the main clause **cannot** be a request, command, invitation, wish or intention:

- Wrong: 駅に着くと、電話してください。
- Right: 駅に着いたら、電話してください。 (Please call me when you arrive at the station.)

For those, use [たら](#/grammar/conditional-tara) or [ば](#/grammar/conditional-ba).

## Comparing the conditionals

| Form | Core idea | Typical use |
| ---- | --------- | ----------- |
| と | automatic, always | nature, machines, directions, habits |
| ば | general condition, focus on the condition | proverbs, advice, hypotheticals |
| たら | once X happens / if X | most flexible, everyday speech |
| なら | if that's the case | responding to context, advice |

## Pitfalls

- と after a verb is conditional, after a noun it means "and" or "with": パンと卵 vs 押すと. See [Particle と](#/grammar/particle-to).
- ないと alone at the end of a sentence means "have to": 行かないと (I've got to go). See [Nakereba naranai](#/grammar/nakereba-naranai).
- Don't use the past tense before と: 押したと is wrong in this meaning.
