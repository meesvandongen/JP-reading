**へ** marks the **direction** of movement: "to" or "toward" a place. Although it is written with the hiragana へ (he), as a particle it is pronounced **e**.

## Formation

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Place + へ + motion verb | 東京へ行く | go to Tokyo |
| Direction + へ | 北へ向かう | head north |
| Person + への + noun | 母への手紙 | a letter to my mother |

Common motion verbs: 行く (go), 来る (come), 帰る (return home), 向かう (head for), 出かける (go out), 引っ越す (move house).

## Examples

- 来月、日本へ行きます。 — I'm going to Japan next month.
- 鳥が南へ飛んでいきました。 — The birds flew away to the south.
- 早く家へ帰りたい。 — I want to go home soon.
- この電車は大阪へ向かっています。 — This train is heading for Osaka.
- ようこそ、日本へ！ — Welcome to Japan!
- 先生への質問はこの紙に書いてください。 — Please write questions for the teacher on this sheet.

## へ vs に

For destinations, へ and に are often interchangeable: 学校へ行く and 学校に行く both mean "go to school". The difference is one of focus:

- **へ** emphasises the **direction** or the journey.
- **に** emphasises the **arrival point**.

In everyday speech, に is more common; へ feels slightly more written or formal. Use に, not へ, when the verb is not about movement (book reservations, existence, giving).

## への

Only へ (not に) can be followed by の to modify a noun: 友達へのプレゼント (a present for a friend), 未来への希望 (hope for the future). に cannot do this: 友達にのプレゼント is wrong.

Similarly, へ can combine with は and も: 京都へは行ったことがありません (Kyoto, I've never been to).

## Pitfalls

- Remember the pronunciation: へ as a particle is **e**, just as は as a particle is **wa**.
- へ does not mark purpose. "Go to see a film" is 映画を見に行く; 見へ行く is wrong.
- Signs and letters often use へ: 出口へ (to the exit), 田中様へ (to Mr. Tanaka).
