The **copula** links a topic to a noun or na-adjective, much like "is" or "am" in English. **です** (desu) is the polite form used with strangers, teachers and in most textbook Japanese. **だ** (da) is the plain form used with friends, in casual writing, and inside longer sentences.

Unlike English "to be", the copula does **not** express existence or location. For "there is" you need the verbs ある (for things) and いる (for people and animals).

## Forms

|                  | Plain        | Polite             |
| ---------------- | ------------ | ------------------ |
| Present          | 学生だ        | 学生です            |
| Negative         | 学生じゃない  | 学生じゃないです / 学生ではありません |
| Past             | 学生だった    | 学生でした          |
| Past negative    | 学生じゃなかった | 学生じゃなかったです / 学生ではありませんでした |

じゃ is the spoken contraction of では. In formal writing では is preferred.

## Examples

- 私は学生です。 — I am a student.
- これは私の本だ。 — This is my book.
- 田中さんは先生ではありません。 — Mr. Tanaka is not a teacher.
- 昨日は雨でした。 — It was rainy yesterday.
- 子供のころ、ここは公園だった。 — When I was a child, this was a park.
- 静かな町ですね。 — It's a quiet town, isn't it?
- 「明日は休み？」「うん、休みだよ。」 — "Are you off tomorrow?" "Yeah, I'm off."

## Notes and pitfalls

**Do not use だ after i-adjectives.** 高いだ is wrong. With i-adjectives, です is only a politeness marker: 高いです is fine, but the plain form is just 高い. See [I-adjectives](#/grammar/i-adjectives).

**だ is often dropped.** In casual speech, especially questions and after sentence-ending particles, だ disappears: 学生？ (Student?), きれいね (Pretty, huh). Women's speech in particular tends to drop it before ね and よ. Bare だ at the end of a sentence can sound blunt or assertive.

**The topic can be anything.** A famous sentence is 僕はうなぎだ, literally "I am eel", which in a restaurant means "I'll have the eel". The copula says "as for X, it's Y" and context fills in the relationship.

**Questions** are made by adding か: 学生ですか. In plain speech, use rising intonation without だ: 学生？ Plain だか is not used to ask questions.

**Formality of じゃないです vs ではありません.** Both are polite. ではありません sounds more formal and written; じゃないです is common in everyday polite conversation.
