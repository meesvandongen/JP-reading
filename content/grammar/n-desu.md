**んです** (spoken) or **のです** (written) after a plain-form clause gives a sentence an **explanatory** tone: "it's that...", "the thing is...". It connects what you say to the situation, a reason, or something both speakers can see. It is extremely common in conversation, and learners who never use it sound stiff.

## Formation

Plain form + んです / のです. Na-adjectives and nouns take **な**.

| Before | Example |
| ------ | ------- |
| Verb | 行くんです, 行ったんです |
| I-adjective | 高いんです |
| Na-adjective | 静か**な**んです |
| Noun | 学生**な**んです |

| Register | Form |
| -------- | ---- |
| Casual | 行くんだ / 行くの |
| Polite | 行くんです |
| Written | 行くのだ / 行くのです |

## Uses

**Giving an explanation or reason**

- 「どうして食べないの？」「お腹が痛いんです。」 — "Why aren't you eating?" "My stomach hurts."
- 遅れてすみません。電車が遅れたんです。 — Sorry I'm late. The train was delayed.

**Asking for an explanation** (about something you've noticed)

- どうしたんですか。 — What's wrong? / What happened?
- 日本語が上手ですね。日本に住んでいたんですか。 — Your Japanese is good. Did you live in Japan?

**Introducing a request or topic** (with けど / が)

- すみません、道を聞きたいんですが…。 — Excuse me, I'd like to ask for directions...
- 来週引っ越すんですけど、手伝ってくれませんか。 — I'm moving next week; could you help me?

**Emphasis, discovery, determination**

- やっぱり彼が犯人だったんだ！ — So he was the culprit after all!
- 絶対に諦めないんだ。 — I'm absolutely not giving up.

## Casual の

In casual speech, の replaces んだ in questions and gentle statements: どこ行くの？ (Where are you going?), 頭が痛いの (I have a headache). Plain statements with の tend to sound soft or feminine; men often use んだ.

## When not to use it

んです implies a context to explain. Using it for simple facts with no background sounds odd or overly emotional: introducing yourself as 私は学生なんです sounds like a confession, while 私は学生です is neutral. Likewise, overusing なんですか can sound like you're demanding an explanation.

## Pitfalls

- Remember な after nouns and na-adjectives: 学生なんです, not 学生んです.
- Questions with んですか are not just questions; they ask "what's the story?". 行きますか asks about plans; 行くんですか shows you've noticed signs that someone is going.
- The negative goes before ん: 行かないんです (the thing is, I'm not going). 行くんじゃない is a different expression, often a scolding "Don't go!".
