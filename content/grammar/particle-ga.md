**が** marks the **grammatical subject** of a verb or adjective, especially when that subject is new information or the focus of the sentence. Where [は](#/grammar/particle-wa) says "as for X", が says "it is X that...".

## Main uses

| Use | Example |
| --- | ------- |
| Subject, new information | 雨が降っています。 |
| Answer to a "who/what" question | 私が行きます。 |
| Existence with ある・いる | 猫がいます。 |
| Object of likes, skills, wants | 寿司が好きです。 |
| Subject inside a modifying clause | 母が作った料理 |
| "but" between clauses | 行きたいですが、時間がありません。 |

The last one is a different が (a conjunction); see [Keredo / ga](#/grammar/keredo-ga).

## Examples

- あ、バスが来た！ — Oh, the bus is here!
- 誰が窓を割ったの？ — Who broke the window?
- 机の上に鍵があります。 — There is a key on the desk.
- 私は猫が好きです。 — I like cats.
- 弟は料理が上手だ。 — My younger brother is good at cooking.
- 頭が痛いです。 — I have a headache. (My head hurts.)
- 友達が貸してくれた本を読んでいます。 — I'm reading a book that my friend lent me.

## Likes, skills and feelings

Several adjectives and verbs take が for what English treats as the object: 好き (like), 嫌い (dislike), 上手 (good at), 下手 (bad at), 分かる (understand), できる (can do), ほしい (want), and the [potential form](#/grammar/potential). So "I understand Japanese" is 日本語が分かります, not 日本語を分かります.

The person who likes or understands is the topic, marked with は: 私は日本語が分かります. The double structure "X は Y が ..." is very common: 象は鼻が長い (Elephants have long trunks).

## Question words

Question words used as the subject always take が, and so does the answer: 誰が来ましたか。田中さんが来ました。 Using は here is ungrammatical.

## Pitfalls

- Neutral descriptions of what you see happening use が: 子供が遊んでいる (Children are playing). Using は would sound like you are contrasting these children with others.
- In relative clauses, が can be replaced by の: 母の作った料理 means the same as 母が作った料理.
- Choosing が for a known topic can sound like you are singling it out: 私が田中です means "I'm the Tanaka (you're looking for)".
