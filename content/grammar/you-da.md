**ようだ** and its casual equivalent **みたいだ** have two main uses:

1. **Inference**: "it seems", "it appears", based on the speaker's own observation or evidence.
2. **Comparison / likeness**: "like", "as if".

## Formation

| Before | ようだ | みたいだ |
| ------ | ------ | -------- |
| Verb | 行くようだ | 行くみたいだ |
| I-adjective | 高いようだ | 高いみたいだ |
| Na-adjective | 静か**な**ようだ | 静かみたいだ |
| Noun | 学生**の**ようだ | 学生みたいだ |

Both behave like **na-adjectives**:

| Use | ようだ | みたいだ |
| --- | ------ | -------- |
| Before a noun | 夢のような話 | 夢みたいな話 |
| As an adverb | 子供のように | 子供みたいに |

## Inference examples

- 誰か来たようです。 — It seems someone has come.
- 田中さんは風邪をひいたみたい。 — It looks like Mr. Tanaka has caught a cold.
- 道が濡れている。夜、雨が降ったようだ。 — The road is wet. It seems it rained in the night.
- このパソコンは壊れているみたいです。 — This computer seems to be broken.

## Comparison examples

- 彼女の手は氷のように冷たかった。 — Her hands were as cold as ice.
- 夢みたいな一日だった。 — It was a dreamlike day.
- 父は子供のように喜んだ。 — My father was as happy as a child.

## Examples / "such as"

ような can also mean "like" in the sense of an example: 寿司のような日本料理 (Japanese food such as sushi).

## ようだ vs そうだ vs らしい

| Pattern | Basis |
| ------- | ----- |
| そうだ (appearance) | what it looks like on the surface: 雨が降りそう |
| ようだ / みたいだ | the speaker's judgement from evidence: 雨が降ったようだ |
| らしい | hearsay or indirect information: 雨が降ったらしい |
| そうだ (hearsay) | explicit report: 雨が降ったそうだ |

## Register

- **ようだ** is neutral to formal, common in writing.
- **みたいだ** is casual and conversational.

## Other uses of ように

The adverbial ように also appears in purpose and wish patterns, which are separate grammar points: 忘れないように (so as not to forget), 日本語が話せるようになった (I've become able to speak Japanese).

## Pitfalls

- Noun + の + ようだ, but noun + みたいだ (no の): 学生のようだ, 学生みたいだ.
- Na-adjectives: 静かなようだ but 静かみたいだ.
- Don't confuse みたい (seems) with the tai-form 見たい (want to see).
