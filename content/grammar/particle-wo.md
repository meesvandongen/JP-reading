**を** marks the **direct object**: the thing that receives the action of the verb. It is pronounced **o**, the same as お; the spelling を exists almost only for this particle.

## Uses

| Use | Pattern | Example |
| --- | ------- | ------- |
| Direct object | Noun + を + verb | パンを食べる |
| Route or space moved through | Place + を + motion verb | 公園を歩く |
| Point of departure | Place + を + leave verb | 家を出る |

## Examples

- 毎朝コーヒーを飲みます。 — I drink coffee every morning.
- 母に手紙を書きました。 — I wrote a letter to my mother.
- 日本語を勉強しています。 — I'm studying Japanese.
- 橋を渡って、右に曲がってください。 — Cross the bridge and turn right.
- 毎晩、犬と川沿いを散歩します。 — I walk along the river with my dog every evening.
- 八時に家を出ました。 — I left home at eight.
- 来年、大学を卒業します。 — I will graduate from university next year.

## Motion verbs

With verbs like 歩く (walk), 走る (run), 渡る (cross), 通る (pass through) and 飛ぶ (fly), を marks the space you cover: 空を飛ぶ (fly through the sky). Compare で, which marks a place where an activity happens: 公園で遊ぶ (play in the park), versus 公園を歩く (walk through the park).

With 出る (leave), 降りる (get off), 卒業する (graduate) and 離れる (leave, separate), を marks the place you leave behind: 電車を降りる. から is also possible with some of these (家から出る), with a more physical "out of" feeling.

## Pitfalls

- Some verbs that are transitive in English take other particles in Japanese. "Meet a friend" is 友達に会う, "ride a train" is 電車に乗る, "understand Japanese" is 日本語が分かる.
- Adjectives and intransitive verbs never take を. ドアが開く (the door opens) vs ドアを開ける (open the door).
- In casual speech を is often dropped: ご飯食べた？ (Did you eat?).
- With する-nouns, you can say 勉強をする or 日本語を勉強する, but not 日本語を勉強をする (two を in one clause is avoided).
- With [tai-form](#/grammar/tai-form), both を and が are possible: 水を飲みたい / 水が飲みたい.
