The **potential form** expresses ability or possibility: 泳げる (can swim), 食べられる (can eat). It is the everyday way to say "can", shorter and more natural in speech than [ことができる](#/grammar/koto-ga-dekiru).

## Formation

| Group | Rule | Example |
| ----- | ---- | ------- |
| Godan | change u-sound to e-sound + る | 書く → 書ける, 話す → 話せる, 読む → 読める, 買う → 買える, 帰る → 帰れる |
| Ichidan | drop る + られる | 食べる → 食べられる, 見る → 見られる |
| する | できる | 勉強する → 勉強できる |
| 来る | 来られる (こられる) | |

The potential form is itself an **ichidan verb**: 書ける → 書けます, 書けない, 書けた.

## Examples

- 私は少し日本語が話せます。 — I can speak a little Japanese.
- 辛いものは食べられません。 — I can't eat spicy food.
- このアプリで電車の時間が調べられる。 — You can look up train times with this app.
- 昨日はよく眠れなかった。 — I couldn't sleep well last night.
- 明日のパーティーに来られますか。 — Can you come to the party tomorrow?
- 子供のころは泳げなかったが、今は泳げる。 — I couldn't swim as a child, but now I can.
- ここから富士山が見えます。 — You can see Mt. Fuji from here.

## Particles: が or を

The object of a potential verb traditionally takes **が**: 日本語が話せる. In modern speech を is also common, especially with longer phrases: 日本語を話せる. Both are acceptable; が is the safer textbook choice.

## Ra-nuki: 見れる

In casual speech, many people drop the ら from ichidan potentials: 見れる instead of 見られる, 来れる instead of 来られる, 食べれる instead of 食べられる. This is called **ら抜き言葉** (ra-dropping). It is widespread, but considered non-standard in formal writing and exams.

## 見える and 聞こえる

For things that are simply visible or audible, Japanese uses the special verbs **見える** and **聞こえる**, rather than 見られる / 聞ける: 窓から海が見える (The sea is visible from the window), 隣の部屋から音楽が聞こえる (I can hear music from the next room).

見られる / 聞ける are used when it is possible because of circumstances: 今夜、テレビで試合が見られる (You can watch the match on TV tonight).

## Pitfalls

- For ichidan verbs, the potential (食べられる) has the same form as the passive. Context tells them apart. See [Passive](#/grammar/passive).
- 分かる (understand) and できる already include "can"; there is no 分かれる in this sense.
- ある and いる have no potential form in normal use.
