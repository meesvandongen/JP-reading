**Masu-stem + やすい** means "easy to do" and **masu-stem + にくい** means "hard to do". The result is an **i-adjective** that describes the thing, not the person: 読みやすい本 (a book that is easy to read).

## Formation

| Verb | Masu-stem | Easy to | Hard to |
| ---- | --------- | ------- | ------- |
| 読む | 読み | 読みやすい | 読みにくい |
| 使う | 使い | 使いやすい | 使いにくい |
| 分かる | 分かり | 分かりやすい | 分かりにくい |
| 食べる | 食べ | 食べやすい | 食べにくい |
| 歩く | 歩き | 歩きやすい | 歩きにくい |

Both conjugate as i-adjectives: 使いやすくない, 使いやすかった, 使いやすくて.

## Examples

- この辞書は使いやすいです。 — This dictionary is easy to use.
- 先生の説明はとても分かりやすかった。 — The teacher's explanation was very easy to understand.
- この靴は歩きやすいから、旅行にぴったりだ。 — These shoes are comfortable to walk in, so they're perfect for travelling.
- 字が小さくて、読みにくい。 — The writing is small and hard to read.
- この薬は苦くて飲みにくい。 — This medicine is bitter and hard to swallow.
- 冬は風邪をひきやすい。 — It's easy to catch a cold in winter.
- 白い服は汚れやすいです。 — White clothes get dirty easily.

## Tendency

With verbs that describe things happening (not deliberate actions), やすい means "tends to", "is prone to": 壊れやすい (fragile, easily broken), 太りやすい (put on weight easily), 忘れやすい (forgetful).

## にくい vs づらい

**づらい** also means "hard to", but with a feeling of discomfort, often physical or emotional: 言いづらい (hard to say, awkward), この椅子は座りづらい (this chair is uncomfortable to sit on). にくい is more neutral and objective. For events that happen by themselves rather than deliberate actions, only にくい works: 壊れにくい (hard to break, durable), not 壊れづらい in careful speech.

## Don't confuse with 易しい / 難しい

易しい (やさしい, easy) and 難しい (difficult) are standalone adjectives describing difficulty in general: この問題は難しい. やすい / にくい attach to a specific verb: 解きにくい問題 (a problem that's hard to solve).

Also note: 安い (cheap) is a different word, even though it's also pronounced やすい.

## Pitfalls

- Use the masu-stem: 読みやすい, not 読むやすい.
- The subject is the thing, with は or が: このペンは書きやすい, not 私はこのペンを書きやすい.
- For people's abilities, use the [potential form](#/grammar/potential) instead.
