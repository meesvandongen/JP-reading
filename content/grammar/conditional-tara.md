**たら** is the most versatile conditional. It means "if" or "when", with the sense of "**once** X has happened, Y". Because it has the fewest restrictions, it is the safest choice for learners and very common in conversation.

## Formation

Plain past (ta-form) + ら.

| Word | Ta-form | Tara-form |
| ---- | ------- | --------- |
| 行く | 行った | 行ったら |
| 食べる | 食べた | 食べたら |
| する | した | したら |
| 行かない | 行かなかった | 行かなかったら |
| 高い | 高かった | 高かったら |
| いい | よかった | よかったら |
| 静か | 静かだった | 静かだったら |
| 雨 | 雨だった | 雨だったら |

## Uses

**1. "When" (certain to happen)**

- 駅に着いたら、電話してください。 — Please call me when you get to the station.
- 仕事が終わったら、飲みに行こう。 — Let's go for a drink when work is over.

**2. "If" (hypothetical)**

- 明日雨が降ったら、試合は中止です。 — If it rains tomorrow, the match is cancelled.
- 宝くじが当たったら、何をしたい？ — What would you want to do if you won the lottery?

**3. Discovery or unexpected result (past)**

- 窓を開けたら、雪が降っていた。 — When I opened the window, it was snowing.
- 薬を飲んだら、すぐ治った。 — I took the medicine and got better right away.

**4. Suggestions with どう / いい**

- 先生に聞いてみたらどうですか。 — Why don't you try asking the teacher?
- よかったら、一緒に行きませんか。 — If you like, would you come with me?

## Why たら is so flexible

Unlike [と](#/grammar/conditional-to), たら can be followed by requests, invitations, commands and wishes. Unlike [ば](#/grammar/conditional-ba), it has no restriction when the main clause is a request. Unlike [なら](#/grammar/conditional-nara), it shows that the condition comes **first** in time.

## たら vs なら: order of events

| Sentence | Meaning |
| -------- | ------- |
| 日本に行ったら、カメラを買います。 | I'll buy a camera **after** arriving in Japan. |
| 日本に行くなら、カメラを買います。 | If I'm going to Japan, I'll buy a camera (before going). |

## Counterfactual

With a past main clause ending in のに or よかった, たら describes things that didn't happen: もっと早く知っていたらよかった (I wish I had known sooner).

## Pitfalls

- たら does not mean past: 着いたら refers to the future here. The た just marks that the condition is completed before the result.
- In general truths and scientific facts, と or ば sounds more natural than たら.
- In casual speech, a bare たら with rising intonation is a suggestion: 少し休んだら？ (Why don't you rest a bit?). たらいいのに expresses a wish: 晴れたらいいのに (I wish it would clear up).
