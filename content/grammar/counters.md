In Japanese you cannot put a number directly before a noun. Instead, you use a **counter** (助数詞) that matches the kind of thing being counted, similar to "three **sheets** of paper" or "two **cups** of tea" in English, but required for everything.

## Common counters

| Counter | Used for | 1 | 2 | 3 | How many? |
| ------- | -------- | - | - | - | --------- |
| つ | general things (up to 10) | 一つ | 二つ | 三つ | いくつ |
| 人 (にん) | people | 一人 (ひとり) | 二人 (ふたり) | 三人 | 何人 |
| 枚 (まい) | flat things: paper, shirts, tickets | 一枚 | 二枚 | 三枚 | 何枚 |
| 本 (ほん) | long thin things: pens, bottles | 一本 (いっぽん) | 二本 | 三本 (さんぼん) | 何本 (なんぼん) |
| 匹 (ひき) | small animals | 一匹 (いっぴき) | 二匹 | 三匹 (さんびき) | 何匹 (なんびき) |
| 冊 (さつ) | books, notebooks | 一冊 (いっさつ) | 二冊 | 三冊 | 何冊 |
| 台 (だい) | machines, vehicles | 一台 | 二台 | 三台 | 何台 |
| 杯 (はい) | cups, glasses, bowls | 一杯 (いっぱい) | 二杯 | 三杯 (さんばい) | 何杯 (なんばい) |
| 個 (こ) | small objects | 一個 (いっこ) | 二個 | 三個 | 何個 |
| 回 (かい) | times, occurrences | 一回 (いっかい) | 二回 | 三回 | 何回 |

## Where the number goes

The most natural pattern puts the number + counter **after the particle**, right before the verb:

**Noun + particle + number-counter + verb**: りんごを三つ買った.

You can also put it before the noun with の, which is more common in writing or when the quantity is a fixed set: 三つのりんご.

## Examples

- りんごを二つください。 — Two apples, please.
- 教室に学生が五人います。 — There are five students in the classroom.
- 切手を十枚買いました。 — I bought ten stamps.
- ビールを一本飲みました。 — I drank a bottle of beer.
- 家に猫が三匹います。 — We have three cats at home.
- 先月、本を何冊読みましたか。 — How many books did you read last month?
- 日本には二回行ったことがあります。 — I've been to Japan twice.

## Sound changes

Many counters change their sound after 1, 3, 6, 8 and 10. Typical patterns: 一本 いっぽん, 三本 さんぼん, 六本 ろっぽん, 八本 はっぽん, 十本 じゅっぽん. Counters starting with h, k, s, t sounds are the ones that change. Learning them by ear with the most common counters is easiest.

## Native numbers with つ

The つ counter uses native Japanese numbers: ひとつ, ふたつ, みっつ, よっつ, いつつ, むっつ, ななつ, やっつ, ここのつ, とお. Above ten, use plain numbers (十一) or 個.

## Pitfalls

- 一人 and 二人 are irregular (ひとり, ふたり); from three it is regular (さんにん).
- When unsure, つ (for objects up to ten) and 個 are safe defaults.
- 一杯 (いっぱい) also means "full" or "a lot".
