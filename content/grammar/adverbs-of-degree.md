**Adverbs of degree** tell you how much: "very", "a little", "not much", "not at all". They come before the adjective or verb they modify. Some of them only work with **negative** sentences, which is the main thing to learn.

## The scale

| Adverb | Meaning | Used with |
| ------ | ------- | --------- |
| 非常に | extremely (formal) | positive |
| とても | very | positive (mostly) |
| すごく | really, super (casual) | positive or negative |
| かなり | quite, considerably | positive |
| 結構 | fairly, pretty (more than expected) | positive |
| 少し / ちょっと | a little | positive |
| あまり | not very, not much | **negative** |
| 全然 | not at all | **negative** |
| 全く | not at all, utterly (formal) | mostly negative |
| もっと | more | positive |
| 一番 | most | positive |

## Examples

- この映画はとても面白かったです。 — This film was very interesting.
- 今日はすごく疲れた。 — I'm really tired today.
- この問題はかなり難しいですね。 — This problem is quite difficult, isn't it?
- このカレー、結構辛いね。 — This curry is pretty spicy, huh.
- 少し休みましょう。 — Let's take a short break.
- 私はあまりテレビを見ません。 — I don't watch TV much.
- 彼の話は全然分からなかった。 — I didn't understand what he said at all.
- もっとゆっくり話してください。 — Please speak more slowly.

## Negative-only adverbs

**あまり** and **全然** need a negative verb or adjective: あまり高くない (not very expensive), 全然知らない (don't know at all).

Saying あまり高い is a typical learner mistake. (In casual modern speech, 全然 is sometimes used with positives, as in 全然大丈夫 "totally fine", but avoid this in formal Japanese.)

## ちょっと as a soft "no"

ちょっと literally means "a little", but on its own it is a polite way to refuse: 「明日はどう？」「明日はちょっと…」 ("How about tomorrow?" "Tomorrow is a bit...") means "No, tomorrow doesn't work."

## とても vs すごく

とても is neutral and works in polite speech and writing. すごく is more casual and emotional. In speech you will also hear めっちゃ (Kansai-origin slang) and 超 (ちょう, slang).

## 一番 and もっと

一番 makes a superlative: 一番好きな食べ物 (my favourite food). もっと means "more": もっと食べたい (I want to eat more).

## Pitfalls

- 結構 also means "no thank you" in 結構です, so context matters.
- とても with a negative (とても食べられない) means "cannot possibly", a different, stronger nuance.
