**Sentence-ending particles** (終助詞) come at the very end of a sentence and add the speaker's attitude: asserting, seeking agreement, musing, emphasising. They are essential for natural spoken Japanese, and they carry much of the "tone" that English expresses with intonation.

## The main particles

| Particle | Function | Rough English |
| -------- | -------- | ------------- |
| よ | tells the listener something new | "you know", "I tell you" |
| ね | seeks or assumes agreement | "isn't it?", "right?" |
| よね | checks shared knowledge | "it's X, right?" |
| か | question | (question mark) |
| の | soft question or explanation | (casual) |
| な / なあ | musing, self-talk; also prohibition | "hmm", "I wish" |
| わ | soft emphasis (feminine) / Kansai assertion | |
| ぞ / ぜ | strong assertion (masculine, rough) | "I'm telling you!" |
| さ | casual, light assertion | "y'know" |
| かな | wondering | "I wonder" |

## Examples

- この店のケーキ、美味しいよ。 — The cake at this shop is good, you know.
- 今日は暑いですね。 — It's hot today, isn't it?
- 明日、休みだよね？ — Tomorrow's a day off, right?
- どこに行くの？ — Where are you going?
- いい天気だなあ。 — What nice weather.
- 彼、来るかな。 — I wonder if he'll come.
- そろそろ行くぞ！ — We're going now!
- 雨が降りそうですよ。傘を持って行ってくださいね。 — It looks like rain. Take an umbrella, OK?

## よ vs ね

The key difference is **who knows the information**.

- **よ**: the speaker knows something the listener doesn't. 電車、もう来ましたよ (The train's already here, just so you know).
- **ね**: both share the information or feeling. いい天気ですね (Nice weather, isn't it?).
- **よね**: the speaker thinks it's true and wants confirmation.

Overusing よ can sound pushy or lecturing, especially to superiors. ね is friendlier, but answering a question about your own life with ね (私は学生ですね) sounds odd, because the listener can't share that knowledge.

## Gender and register

Some particles carry gender or roughness associations, though modern usage is flexible. ぞ and ぜ sound masculine and rough. わ with rising intonation sounds feminine in Tokyo speech, while in Kansai, わ is used by everyone. Learners are safest with よ, ね, よね and かな.

## Combining with だ

After nouns and na-adjectives in casual speech, だ often comes before よ (学生だよ) but may be dropped before ね (学生ね). Before か, the casual question drops it entirely: 学生？

## Pitfalls

- な after a dictionary form is a **prohibition**: 触るな！ (Don't touch!). See [Imperative](#/grammar/imperative).
- Polite speech can and should still use ね and よ: そうですね, 大丈夫ですよ.
