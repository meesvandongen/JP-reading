**のに** means "even though", "despite the fact that". Unlike the neutral けど, it carries the speaker's **emotion**: surprise, frustration, disappointment or regret that the result goes against expectations.

## Formation

Plain form + のに. Na-adjectives and nouns take **な**.

| Before | Example |
| ------ | ------- |
| Verb | 勉強したのに |
| I-adjective | 高いのに |
| Na-adjective | 静か**な**のに |
| Noun | 日曜日**な**のに |

## Examples

- 一生懸命勉強したのに、試験に落ちた。 — Even though I studied really hard, I failed the exam.
- 日曜日なのに、会社に行かなければならない。 — Even though it's Sunday, I have to go to work.
- 約束したのに、彼は来なかった。 — He promised, but he didn't come.
- もう四月なのに、まだ寒いですね。 — It's already April, but it's still cold, isn't it?
- 何度も説明したのに、まだ分からないの？ — I've explained it so many times, and you still don't get it?

## Sentence-final のに: regret

At the end of a sentence, のに expresses regret or a wish about something that didn't happen: "if only...".

- 言ってくれればよかったのに。 — You should have told me. (If only you'd told me.)
- もっと時間があればいいのに。 — I wish I had more time.
- せっかく作ったのに。 — And I went to all the trouble of making it...

## のに vs けど / が

| のに | けど / が |
| ---- | --------- |
| emotional: surprise, complaint | neutral contrast |
| main clause is a fact (what actually happened) | main clause can be anything |
| cannot be followed by a request, command, wish or intention | no restriction |

So 雨が降っているけど、出かけよう (It's raining, but let's go out) is fine, while 雨が降っているのに、出かけよう is wrong.

## Purpose: "in order to"

A different のに after the **dictionary form** means "for (the purpose of)", usually with words like 使う, 便利, 必要, 時間がかかる. For example, このナイフは魚を切るのに便利です means "This knife is handy for cutting fish", and 家を建てるのにお金がたくさん必要だ means "You need a lot of money to build a house".

Context makes it clear which のに is meant.

## Pitfalls

- Don't forget な: 学生なのに, 静かなのに.
- のに about other people's actions can sound like a complaint, so be careful in polite situations.
- Don't confuse with ので (because): 雨なので (because it's rain) vs 雨なのに (even though it's rain).
