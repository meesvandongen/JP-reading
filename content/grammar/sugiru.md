**すぎる** means "to exceed". Attached to verbs and adjectives, it means **too much**, **over-**: 食べすぎる (overeat), 高すぎる (too expensive). It almost always implies a negative result.

## Formation

| Type | Rule | Example |
| ---- | ---- | ------- |
| Verb | masu-stem + すぎる | 飲む → 飲みすぎる |
| I-adjective | drop い + すぎる | 高い → 高すぎる |
| Na-adjective | stem + すぎる | 静か → 静かすぎる |
| ない (adjective) | なさすぎる | 時間がなさすぎる |
| いい | よすぎる | よすぎる |

すぎる is an **ichidan verb**: すぎます, すぎた, すぎて, すぎない. The noun form すぎ is also common: 飲みすぎ (excessive drinking), 働きすぎ (overwork).

## Examples

- 昨日はお酒を飲みすぎて、頭が痛い。 — I drank too much yesterday, so I have a headache.
- この靴は高すぎて、買えません。 — These shoes are too expensive for me to buy.
- この部屋は静かすぎて、ちょっと怖い。 — This room is too quiet; it's a bit scary.
- 説明が難しすぎて、全然分からなかった。 — The explanation was too difficult; I didn't understand a thing.
- 働きすぎに気をつけてください。 — Be careful not to overwork.
- 昨日、ゲームをしすぎてしまった。 — I ended up playing games too much yesterday.
- このカレー、辛すぎない？ — Isn't this curry too spicy?

## Common pairing: すぎて + result

すぎて often gives a reason: 重すぎて持てない (too heavy to carry), 眠すぎて集中できない (too sleepy to concentrate). This matches English "too X to Y".

## Positive use

In modern casual speech, すぎる can be used as emphasis with positive adjectives: かわいすぎる (way too cute), 美味しすぎる (so delicious). This is slangy and enthusiastic.

## Negatives

With negative adjectives and ない, use **なさすぎる**: 元気がなさすぎる (too lacking in energy). For verb negatives, ない usually becomes なさすぎ as well: 食べなさすぎ (eating far too little). Common words like 少なすぎる and 危なすぎる are just regular i-adjectives (少ない, 危ない) and use なすぎる.

## Pitfalls

- Use the masu-stem with verbs: 食べすぎる, not 食べるすぎる.
- Not every "too" in English uses すぎる. "Me too" is 私も; "also" uses も.
- 過ぎる as an independent verb means "pass (time or place)": 十時を過ぎた (it's past ten).
