**の** connects two nouns, with the first one describing the second. The basic meaning is possession ("my book"), but it covers almost any relationship: material, origin, location, category, time.

## Formation

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- | ------- |
| Noun + の + noun | 私の本 | my book |
| Place + の + noun | 日本の車 | a Japanese car |
| Time + の + noun | 朝のニュース | the morning news |
| Noun + の (noun omitted) | これは私のです | this is mine |
| Adjective/verb + の | 赤いのがいい | I'd like the red one |

The order is the reverse of English "of": **X の Y** = "Y of X". The main noun always comes last.

## Examples

- これは姉の傘です。 — This is my older sister's umbrella.
- 東京の大学で経済を勉強しています。 — I study economics at a university in Tokyo.
- 日本語の先生はとても優しいです。 — My Japanese teacher is very kind.
- 机の上に猫がいます。 — There is a cat on the desk.
- 「この鍵は誰の？」「私の。」 — "Whose key is this?" "Mine."
- もっと大きいのはありませんか。 — Don't you have a bigger one?
- 友達の山田さんを紹介します。 — Let me introduce my friend, Ms. Yamada.

## Position words

Location words like 上 (top), 下 (under), 中 (inside), 前 (front), 後ろ (behind), 横 (beside) are nouns, so they attach with の: 駅の前 (in front of the station), 箱の中 (inside the box).

## Apposition

With people, の can mean "who is": 友達の山田さん is "Yamada, my friend", not "my friend's Yamada". Context decides.

## Other uses of の

- **Pronoun "one":** after adjectives and clauses, の replaces a noun: 安いのを買った (I bought the cheap one).
- **Nominalizer:** turns a verb phrase into a noun: 泳ぐのが好き. See [Nominalizers の and こと](#/grammar/nominalizers-no-koto).
- **Explanation:** the の in のです / んです. See [The explanatory んです](#/grammar/n-desu).
- **Casual question:** at the end of a sentence, a soft question: どこに行くの？
- **Subject in relative clauses:** 母の作った料理 = 母が作った料理.

## Pitfalls

- Na-adjectives take な, not の: 静かな部屋, not 静かの部屋. A few words (病気, 普通) behave like nouns and take の.
- i-adjectives never take の: 高い車, not 高いの車.
- Long chains are fine in Japanese: 私の友達の兄の車 (my friend's older brother's car).
