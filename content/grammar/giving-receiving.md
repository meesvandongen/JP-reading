Japanese has three verbs for giving and receiving, and the choice depends on **who gives to whom** relative to the speaker. They are also used after the te-form for doing **favours**.

## The three verbs

| Verb | Direction | Pattern |
| ---- | --------- | ------- |
| あげる | I / my side → others; others → others | Giver は Receiver に X をあげる |
| くれる | others → me / my side | Giver は (私に) X をくれる |
| もらう | receive from someone | Receiver は Giver に／から X をもらう |

The key rule: **くれる is only for giving toward the speaker** (or the speaker's family and group). あげる is never used for things given to you.

## Polite and humble versions

| Plain | Humble / honorific |
| ----- | ------------------ |
| あげる | さしあげる (to a superior), やる (to animals, plants, juniors) |
| くれる | くださる (from a superior) |
| もらう | いただく (from a superior) |

## Examples

- 私は友達に誕生日プレゼントをあげました。 — I gave my friend a birthday present.
- 姉が私にマフラーをくれた。 — My older sister gave me a scarf.
- 私は先生から辞書をもらいました。 — I received a dictionary from my teacher.
- 母は毎朝、花に水をやる。 — My mother waters the flowers every morning.
- 田中さんが道を教えてくれました。 — Mr. Tanaka kindly showed me the way.
- 友達に引っ越しを手伝ってもらった。 — I had a friend help me move.
- 妹の宿題を見てあげた。 — I helped my younger sister with her homework.

## Favours: てあげる, てくれる, てもらう

After the te-form, these verbs show that an action is done **for someone's benefit**:

| Pattern | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- |
| てあげる | I do something for someone |
| てくれる | someone does something for me (and I appreciate it) |
| てもらう | I get / have someone do something for me |

Japanese speakers use てくれる and てもらう naturally to show gratitude. Saying 友達が手伝った (my friend helped) is grammatical but sounds oddly cold; 手伝ってくれた expresses that you benefited.

## Requests

These verbs form many polite requests: 手伝ってくれる？ (casual), 手伝ってくれませんか, 手伝ってもらえませんか, 手伝っていただけませんか (very polite).

## Pitfalls

- **てあげる can sound condescending** when said directly to the person you're helping, especially a superior: 先生、持ってあげます is rude. Use the humble お持ちします instead.
- With もらう, the giver is marked with に or から; with くれる and あげる, に marks the receiver.
- "My mother gave my friend a present" uses あげる, but "My friend gave my mother a present" uses くれる, because your mother is on your side.
