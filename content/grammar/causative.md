The **causative form** means **making** or **letting** someone do something: 子供に野菜を食べさせる (make the child eat vegetables), 子供を遊ばせる (let the children play). Whether it's "make" or "let" depends on context.

## Formation

| Group | Rule | Example |
| ----- | ---- | ------- |
| Godan | change u-sound to a-sound + せる | 書く → 書かせる, 行く → 行かせる, 待つ → 待たせる, 言う → 言わせる |
| Ichidan | drop る + させる | 食べる → 食べさせる, 見る → 見させる |
| する | させる | 勉強する → 勉強させる |
| 来る | 来させる (こさせる) | |

The causative is an ichidan verb: 書かせます, 書かせた, 書かせない. In casual speech, a short form also exists for godan verbs: 書かす, 行かす.

## Particles: に or を

The person made / allowed to act is the **causee**.

| Verb type | Causee | Example |
| --------- | ------ | ------- |
| Transitive (has an object) | に | 母は私に部屋を掃除させた |
| Intransitive | を (forced) or に (allowed) | 子供を走らせた / 子供に走らせた |

With intransitive verbs, を suggests forcing, while に suggests the person wanted to and was allowed.

## Examples

- 母は弟に野菜を食べさせた。 — My mother made my younger brother eat vegetables.
- 先生は学生に作文を書かせました。 — The teacher had the students write an essay.
- 子供たちを公園で自由に遊ばせた。 — I let the children play freely in the park.
- 待たせてしまって、すみません。 — Sorry to have kept you waiting.
- 彼の冗談はいつもみんなを笑わせる。 — His jokes always make everyone laugh.
- 父は私を留学させてくれた。 — My father let me study abroad.

## Asking permission: させてください

**Causative te-form + ください** means "please let me": 私に払わせてください (Please let me pay). Very common and polite:

- 少し考えさせてください。 — Please let me think about it a little.
- 今日は早く帰らせていただけませんか。 — Could you possibly let me go home early today?

## させていただく

In business Japanese, させていただきます (humbly do, with your permission) is everywhere: 本日は休業させていただきます (We are closed today). See [Kenjougo](#/grammar/keigo-kenjougo).

## Making vs letting

Adding てくれる or てもらう makes it clear that it's "letting" (a favour): 行かせてくれた (let me go). Without them, it often sounds like "making".

## Pitfalls

- Using the causative on superiors is rude: 社長に書類を見させる sounds like you're ordering the president. Use お願いする or ていただく instead.
- Causatives of emotion verbs mean "cause to feel": 親を心配させる (make one's parents worry), 驚かせる (surprise).
