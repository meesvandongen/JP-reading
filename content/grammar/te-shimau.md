**てしまう** (te-form + しまう) has two related meanings:

1. **Completion**: doing something all the way, finishing it off.
2. **Regret or accident**: something happened that you didn't want, or did unintentionally. English "ended up", "accidentally", or just a sigh.

## Formation

Te-form + しまう. しまう is a godan verb.

| | Formal | Casual (て → ちゃ, で → じゃ) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | 食べてしまう | 食べちゃう |
| Past | 食べてしまった | 食べちゃった |
| Polite past | 食べてしまいました | 食べちゃいました |
| 飲む | 飲んでしまう | 飲んじゃう |
| 行く | 行ってしまった | 行っちゃった |

## Examples

**Completion**

- 宿題はもうやってしまいました。 — I've already finished my homework.
- この本は一日で読んでしまった。 — I read this whole book in one day.
- 早く食べちゃいなさい。 — Hurry up and finish eating.

**Regret or accident**

- 電車に傘を忘れてしまいました。 — I left my umbrella on the train (oops).
- 寝坊して、遅刻してしまった。 — I overslept and ended up being late.
- ごめん、ケーキを全部食べちゃった。 — Sorry, I ate all the cake.
- 大事な花瓶を割ってしまった。 — I broke an important vase.

## Which meaning?

Often both are present: 全部食べてしまった can be "I finished it all off" or "I (regrettably) ate it all". Context and tone decide. With things that are clearly bad (忘れる, 落とす, 失くす, 壊す), the regret reading is natural.

With intransitive verbs describing things outside your control, it shows that the result is unfortunate: 猫が死んでしまった (My cat died), バスが行ってしまった (The bus has gone, and I missed it).

## Casual contractions

In speech, ちゃう / じゃう are far more common than the full form:

| Full | Casual |
| ---- | ------ |
| てしまう | ちゃう |
| でしまう | じゃう |
| てしまった | ちゃった |
| でしまった | じゃった |

## Future use

てしまう in non-past can express "I'll get it done" or a worry that something will happen: 明日までに終わらせてしまおう (Let's get it done by tomorrow), このままだと遅れてしまう (At this rate we'll end up late).

## Pitfalls

- ちゃう is not the Kansai ちゃう (= 違う, "that's not right"). Context makes it clear.
- Using てしまう for an action you planned and are happy about can sound odd unless you mean "finished off".
- Don't confuse 行っちゃった (has gone off) with 行った (went).
